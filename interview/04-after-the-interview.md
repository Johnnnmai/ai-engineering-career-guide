# After the Interview

The interview ended. You walked out of the room or closed the video call. Now what.

## Send a Thank You Note

Send a brief email to the recruiter (and interviewers if you have their contact) within 24 hours. Keep it to 3-4 sentences. Mention one specific topic from the conversation that you found interesting. Do not recap the entire interview or try to correct an answer you got wrong.

## Debrief While It Is Fresh

Write down what happened within an hour of the interview ending. Record:
- What questions were asked (as precisely as you can remember)
- Where you felt strong and where you stumbled
- System design topics and how deep they probed
- Any signals about the team, culture, or role that surprised you

This debrief is valuable whether you get the offer or not. If you get rejected, it tells you what to work on. If you get the offer, it informs negotiation. If you interview at similar companies later, these notes become your preparation material.

## Waiting for the Decision

Most companies take 3-7 business days after the final round. If you have not heard back after a week, it is appropriate to send one follow-up email to the recruiter asking for a timeline update.

Do not stop interviewing while you wait. Continue applying and preparing for other opportunities. Having multiple active processes gives you leverage and reduces the emotional weight of any single decision.

## If You Get the Offer

Read the full offer letter carefully. Compare total compensation (base, equity, bonus, benefits) rather than just base salary. Ask questions about anything unclear: vesting schedule, team placement, start date flexibility, remote work policy.

If you are going to negotiate, do it promptly and professionally. See the negotiation section in the Getting Hired guide for specifics.

## If You Get Rejected

Ask for feedback. Most recruiters will share general feedback: "The team felt your system design needed more depth" or "The coding round was below our bar." This feedback is gold. Use it.

Rejection from one company does not predict rejection from others. Different companies test different things at different depths. A rejection from Google's ML theory round does not mean you will fail Anthropic's system design round.

Wait 6-12 months before re-applying to the same company. Use that time to address the specific gaps that caused the rejection.

For more on turning rejections into preparation improvements and managing multiple interview pipelines, see [The 0→1 AI Engineer Interview Playbook](https://valenx.org/books/aie).
